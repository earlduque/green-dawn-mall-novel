## Jordan
### *from The Journal of a Mall Explorer, checked in and not checked out*

I am starting this on a clean page because I want the record to begin properly, and because if I am right about any of this then somebody is going to read it, and I would like them to be able to follow along.

Here is what I have. A man at a camera fair in September, sixty-odd, selling lens caps out of a biscuit tin, told me about a camcorder that films what you feel instead of what you see. He said it like a fact about the weather. I asked him three times and he gave me the same answer three times with the same words in it, which is either the truth or a story he has told so often it has set.

He said it was in a mall. He said which mall the way you'd give somebody directions to a post office.

I know how this sounds. I have written up two church fêtes and a planning dispute this month. I am aware of the ratio.

But think about what it would mean. Not a lie detector — those only tell you that somebody's hands are sweating. A camera that records the *feeling*. You could point it at a man behind a desk and play the tape back and there would be no argument left to have. Nobody could ever lie again, not on the record, not once. Every single thing I have ever failed to prove.

I told Alex I was going out to the mall to look at a rumour. She said take an afternoon. I did not tell her the rest of it, because I wanted to come back with the thing and not with the plan.

---

The annex is attached at the north end, past the pretzel place, through the doors with the bar on them.

Fourth floor is offices. Two of the suites are empty and the third one has the blinds down. Nobody is up here. Nobody has been up here for a while — the carpet at the far end is newer than the carpet at the near end, and I don't know what that means yet, but I have written it down.

There is a lift at the end of the corridor that is not on the directory and is not on the fire plan.

I want to be clear that I have thought about this. I have stood here for eleven minutes. The stairs are behind me and they work and they go down to a car park with my bike in it. I have a notebook and a camera and half a flask of tea and nobody in the world knows where I am, which is my own fault, and which I am going to fix by writing it here.

I am going to get in the lift.

It smells of plastic. There's music somewhere, a little piano thing, quite cheerful, quite bad. There are more buttons than the building has floors, which is the single most interesting fact I have encountered in eleven months of employment.

I'll find it in a week. Two at the outside.
