## Jordan
### *from The Journal of a Mall Explorer, checked in and not checked out*

Level Two. I have stopped saying "the second floor" because it is not the second floor. It is above the third one. The signs are perfectly clear about it and I have decided to believe the signs.

Things I know now that I did not know on the first day:

One. Down is an escalator and up is a staircase, and the staircase is there, and I have been up it twice, and both times it came out on Level One and Level One had a lift in it that does not open any more. I am not making a point about being trapped. I am describing the layout.

Two. The light does not go off. Not dimmer, not warmer, not later. I have slept twice and both times I woke up in the same afternoon.

Three. Everything here is thirty years out of date and everything here is brand new, and both of those are true at the same time, and nobody I ask finds it strange.

Four. Nobody I ask is unkind. That is the part I was not ready for. I have spoken to nine people and every one of them was pleasant and busy and could not help me.

---

There is a bookshop on this floor called PageTurn and it only takes books in. It does not lend. There is a man in a hood who works there and he wears gloves, and when I asked him whether that was a rule he said it had worked out fine for him, which is not an answer, and which I have thought about a great deal since.

I gave him the first notebook. The full one, from the first three days, with the fountain in it and the drawing of the ceiling.

I want to be honest about why. It was not generosity. I wanted to know what the shop did.

It came back an hour later on a trolley as a hardback. Green cloth boards. A label on the spine with a number on it. My handwriting had become type, in a face I have never chosen, and the little diagram of the ceiling had been redrawn by somebody with a steadier hand than mine, and it was better, and on the inside of the back cover there was a blank rectangle where they put the photograph of the author.

My name was underneath it. Printed. Spelled right.

I could not check it out. I asked. He was sorry.

---

So that is what this place does. It does not take things away from you. It takes things off you and puts them on a shelf and improves them slightly and then it will not give them back, and it is polite about it the entire time, and it has your name spelled right.

I went and stood in the plaza for a while after that.

The camcorder is real. I have had two people confirm it and one of them described the strap. It is down, past where the escalators stop. There is a room where all of the walls are screens and it is in there.

I am going to write down the other thing now because I have been not writing it down for a day and a half and that is the sort of decision that gets people

My right hand is warm.

Only the right one. Only the palm and the inside of the fingers, and it is not a temperature, it is the *idea* of warmth, the way a room is warm when somebody has just left it. I have held it against the tile and the tile is cold and the hand is warm and the hand is the thing that is wrong.

It is probably nothing. I have been carrying a camera in it for eleven days.
