## Jordan
### *developed*

I am starting this on a clean page, at a desk, in a room with a window in it, and the light is coming through the window from the sun, which is in the sky, which is where it goes.

I start a lot of things this way now. It means I know where I am.

They each asked me, separately, in the first week, what I wanted. Alex asked first, in the car, with the receipt still in her hand. I said I wanted a bath and a month without a screen in it, and that was true. What I did not say, because I did not have it yet, was that I wanted to know what happens to everybody. So here it is, in order, the way you put the cups back on the shelf with the labels facing out.

---

Djan, first, because he went through the door first.

He went back to the darkroom and he has never since developed a photograph without the person in it standing in the room. He calls it manners. The seven Polaroids hung for a season in a gallery above a launderette under the title *The Seven Polaroids*, and people drove in from two towns over to stand in front of them, and nobody could explain why the prints were warm. I can't either. I have put my palm flat on the glass. They are about the temperature of a mug you have been holding for a while and forgot to drink.

He still balances stones on the wall in his garden every morning. He told me — Djan told me, unprompted, which I want on the record — that every so often he comes out and one of them is already balanced, and he leaves that one alone.

He tells me what he sees now. All of it. It takes a while. I have learned to bring a sandwich.

---

Burt was offered head of road crew and turned it down, because the hard hat kept telling him he was overqualified. He opened a lunch counter across the road from the paper. Nine stools, a radio on, soup that changes and bread that doesn't. I have asked around, as a reporter, properly, with a notebook, and I can tell you that nobody who has eaten there has ever felt lost. Not once. Not even the man from the council.

I asked him where the voice came from. The foreman voice, the one that makes everybody in a room about a third better at whatever they are doing. He looked at me with enormous patience and handed me a plate.

---

Stacy was back on air the following Monday without a hair out of place. *Stacy Investigates* ran eleven seasons. She does not use cards on camera. She does not use them off camera either, much, though the deck is in the bag.

The bag is the Neverfull, and it still won't let anybody else unzip it. I tried once, very politely, and the zip would not move under my thumb, and Stacy said *she's particular,* and that was the end of the conversation. The camcorder is in there. It sleeps. I don't want it back, and I have never once asked.

Sometimes, in the middle of a broadcast, she gets a look — small, a quarter of a second — as if somebody has said something in her earpiece. I know what it said.

---

Hank cut the tape into forty minutes, and he did it with me in the room, every hour of it, because he had said yes, and because I asked him after the first day to stop checking whether I was sure. It went to festivals. Audiences cried and could not tell you why, and he sat at the back of every screening with his arms folded and watched them do it and did not film them. Three studios called. He said no three times and went back to tuning cars, and these days he travels with a racing team, in the pit, listening for the hitch at the end of a bar that nobody else can hear.

The magnet is on his fridge. The tape is in a drawer, and on the label, in a hand neither of us knows, it still says FOR JORDAN.

---

Alex runs the paper. I am not going to say what it's called; she says names are how you get letters.

The receipt is framed on the wall of her office, above the desk, where other people put a degree. *ONE (1) INTERN. SLIGHTLY USED.* Whether this means she owns me has never been settled. She says we'll figure it out later, and it has been later for a long time now.

Every few years a résumé comes in. Green blazer. A left hand that never set right. Three hundred years of experience in security. Alex reads it all the way to the bottom, every time, and writes back two words: *You're hired.* She has done it four times. I don't know where the letters go.

But what she is proudest of, and anybody at the paper will tell you this, is the karate. She teaches Tuesdays and Thursdays in a church hall and people fly in. There is a waiting list. There is also a line of gaffer tape, designed by her, for combat purposes, and hers is purple now, and some of the rolls have spikes on, and I own one, and it is very good for jars, which I do not need help with.

---

I wrote the story.

It ran on the front page under a headline nobody believed, next to a photograph nobody could explain, which was the five of them outside the office, squinting into the sun.

I kept the arm. It is smooth and hard-wearing and I would not have chosen the colour. It is excellent for opening jars and terrible for high-fives, and I have stopped apologising to people who go for the high-five.

I have not touched a camcorder since. I take Polaroids. I don't hurry them. I hold them up and wait for them to come in, brown first and then the colour from the edges, and I make sure I am in the room.

---

Two things I have not been able to put on the shelf.

Somebody wrote COME DOWN on the doors, low, in blue ballpoint. I have asked everybody. Djan says not him, and Djan doesn't lie, he only leaves things out. I did not write it. I am almost sure I did not write it.

And there was somebody in a coat, with a bad haircut, who came in for me before anyone else did and went as far as they could and turned back. I have never found them. I would like to. I would like to say thank you, because they came, and coming is the part that costs.

---

Sometimes, in a supermarket, there is a little piano thing over the speakers, a climb and a fall, and every part of me stops.

It is never it. It is always some other song. I stand in the aisle and wait for it to go round again, and it doesn't.

*Have a good day,* the girl at the till says.

I tell her I will.
